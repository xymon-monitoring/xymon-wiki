# Backport tracker rules

These rules govern the two `xymon` backport trackers: [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) (Terabithia patches) and [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) (`devel` commits). Both point here instead of keeping a copy; this page is the only one.

**Every mark has exactly one defining clause.** box → *Checkbox* · icon → *Status icon* · verdict → *Verdict* · `needs <id>` → *Sequencing* · `_(cond)_`, `PARKED`, `no longer applies cleanly` → *Marks that sit beside a verdict* · ⚠ → *⚠ is emphasis*. **A mark with no clause is not a mark:** define it, or stop using it.

**Scope.** [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) tracks **Terabithia patches**; [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) tracks **`devel` commits**. **One line = one artifact, always** — one patch, or one commit. **A bundle is a group, not a line**: give it a heading, put each member on its own line beneath it, and let the heading carry what is true of all of them (see *Grouping* and *Group headings*). Analysis true of only some members is written once, on one of them, and **referenced** from the others (`see <id>`) — never restated, per *No repetition*. Dependencies are expressed the same way — see *Sequencing*. The same underlying change may appear on both — as a patch on [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29), as a commit on [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) — linked by cross-reference, never by ownership.

*Sub-items:* a bullet that only elaborates its parent (a step, a caveat, a sub-task) is **not** a separate artifact and needs no verdict, checkbox or cross-reference of its own.

**Delegation — one way only: [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) → [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29).** When a `devel` commit has a Terabithia twin, [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) **delegates it to [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29)** (verdict 5 below defines the marker). The substantive verdict, its reason, the checkbox and every supporting detail then live on the [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) line. **[#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) never delegates to [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106).**

*Split lines:* where one `devel` commit bundles a Terabithia-derived slice with unrelated work, the line carries the **marker for the delegated slice** and a **verdict plus checkbox for the remainder it still owns** — and says which is which.

*Consequence:* [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106)'s checkboxes cover **devel-only** work — commits with no Terabithia twin. A `backport/devel-*` group may be partly or wholly delegated; where every member is delegated, the group is a reference section, not [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) work.

**Prefer the form that already exists.** Terabithia and `devel` patches are the **source**, not the target: where a corrected version of the same change is already on `main`, or in an open PR ready for review, **that form is what we take** — and the artifact this line tracks becomes a **reference**, not the work: the Terabithia patch on [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29), the `devel` commit on [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106). This is why 🟢 reports the outcome rather than the resemblance, and why the carrier is the undrafted PR.

**Verdict — exactly one per item line, required: one of the five below and nothing else.** (A group heading is not an item line: it carries no verdict, no checkbox and no icon of its own.)

1. **`drop — <reason>`** — we don't want this change on `main`.
   *Reasons:* already in `main` · obsolete / dead platform · N/A on `main` (no effect, no callers) · harmful as written · incomplete as written · superseded · workaround for a problem fixed elsewhere · site-specific tuning, not general · build churn, better done natively · packaging or debug — downstream's business · *other — state it*.
2. **`take as is`** — port it as written.
3. **`take, not as written — <reason>`** — we want the change, not this form.
   *Reasons:* needs improvement (bugs, docs, style) · a different version exists elsewhere — on the other tracker, on `main`, or in an open PR — compare and take the best (**name where it is**, and say whether it covers this item **fully or partly** — the judgement is about the **feature**, not the patch. Three ways to be fully covered: it does the same thing; it does it **better** (a rewrite covers the feature completely and may share no text at all); or it omits a part we have **decided we do not want** — name that part and why. Only a *wanted*, unimplemented part is a gap, and a partly-covered line must say which part is missing. Shared-line counts are evidence, never the measure; an unlocated "a better version exists" is not a reason) · only part of it is wanted · needs a prerequisite first · *other — state it*.
4. **`undecided`** — not triaged yet. Write the word; don't leave it blank.
5. **`delegated → [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) <N>`** — **[#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) only.** Fills the verdict slot **without deciding anything**. A delegated line then carries exactly three things and nothing else: its **identity** (hash and commit subject), this **marker**, and any **group heading** above it. No reason, no checkbox, no icon, no analysis — the substantive verdict and every detail live on the [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) line. Never used on [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29), since delegation is one-way.

**Marks that sit beside a verdict.** Each answers a different question, and none of them replaces the verdict.

- **`_(cond: <gate>)_` — a spec gate.** Terabithia ships the patch only under an RPM condition; the gate names which one. In use: *stable-build gate* · *trunk-variant twin — fold onto its stable pair* · *feature-gated — needs an in-tree gate*. It describes the patch's origin, never our decision.
- **`PARKED` — priority.** Deliberately not being worked on now. It says nothing about whether we want the change.
- **`no longer applies cleanly` — a measurement.** The patch does not apply to `main` at `--fuzz=0`, as of the date given. Context drift, not a verdict: a change can still be wanted and hand-ported.

**Checkbox — its presence is the default hypothesis that a PR will be needed.**

- **`[ ]`** — a PR is expected here; none carries it yet. Default for every `take …` and `undecided` line.
- **`[x]`** — a PR carries it, linked on the line. That PR is the **carrier**; any other PR named on the line is context, and a **dependency is only ever `needs <id>`** — never a bare PR reference. Carrier and dependency are different roles: an item ships *with* its carrier and *after* its dependencies. *Choosing among carriers.* Where more than one PR would carry an item, the carrier is the one **ready for review**: an undrafted PR over a draft. A draft proposes how the work might be done; an undrafted PR offers to land it. **Name every live PR that would carry it** — the chosen one as the carrier, the rest as context — so a reader sees each route the change could take. One closed without merging, or one that has since dropped the change, is **not** named: it carries nothing. Between two of equal standing, prefer the one scoped to this item alone. A tick does **not** mean merged — that is 🟢's job, on both trackers. *A PR settles a line two ways:* by **carrying** its change, or by **removing the need for it**. So a `drop — superseded` line takes `[x]` and names the PR that superseded it, rather than a plain point — the question is resolved, and the reader can see by what. The icon is unaffected: it reports where *this line's* change is, and a superseded change never reaches `main`, so such a line never becomes 🟢 however its superseder ends up. **A supersede is not settled until its superseder lands:** while that PR is open the drop is conditional — if the PR is abandoned the change is wanted again — so the line keeps a priority bucket and **`needs <that PR>`**, and only moves to *Settled* once the PR merges.
- **plain point `-`** — no PR is involved: the line is delegated to [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29), or it is a `drop` we reached on our own judgement with no PR resolving it.

**Cross-reference.** Name the counterpart on the other tracker — the `devel` commit for a [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) line, the Terabithia patch for a [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) line — **when one exists**. Write *not traced* when a counterpart is suspected but unconfirmed. A counterpart is in exactly one of three states: **named** · **not traced** (suspected, unconfirmed) · **none** — and *none* is written only once it has been measured, saying when and against what. **Silence means the question has not been asked**, not that the answer is no. Where both exist and differ, **name both and take the best of the two**; assert that one form wins only once the comparison is recorded. The comparison itself is recorded on the **owning** line — a delegated line carries only what verdict 5 allows. On [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) the counterpart is named by the delegation marker; this clause's [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) → Terabithia direction applies only to a line that is not delegated, or only partly (a split). Cite as *Citations* requires.

**No repetition.** The **substantive** verdict (values 1–4 of *Verdict*; 5 is the pointer), its reason, the checkbox and the analysis for a change live on exactly **one** line — on [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) when it has a Terabithia twin, on [#106](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/106) otherwise. `delegated → [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29)` is a **pointer, not a second verdict**. The delegating side keeps only what verdict 5 allows; anything it knows that the owning line lacks is **moved there**, never duplicated.

*Say it once — within a line too:* **no mark may restate another, and no prose may restate a mark.** If a value can be derived from the marks already present, it is a *reading*, not a mark — do not add it. Where a line carries a mark, the surrounding text must add something the mark does not say — *which* part, *which* symbol, *why* — or say nothing at all. "requires `65`" beside **needs `65`** is a repetition; "(its keywords)" is not. The same holds **within the prose**: no fact twice on one line. Three tests make this checkable — a line fails if any holds:
  1. **the mark's own words reappear in the prose** — if the verdict is `drop — superseded`, the prose says *by what*, never "superseded" again;
  2. **an id appears twice in the same role** — one PR named twice as the carrier, one commit named twice as the twin;
  3. **a relationship is explained twice** — "reimplementation, not a cherry-pick" said in two places.
  Keep the more precise statement and delete the other. This is what retired ⏳ (it meant `[x]` + not-yet-in-`main`) and 🔴 (it meant "no 🟡/🟢"), and why `delegated → [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29)` is a pointer rather than a second verdict. When two fields would carry the same fact, keep the one whose dimension owns it and delete the other.

**Status icon — where the change already is, and nothing else.** Used on **both** trackers: 🟡 **partly in `main`** — some of it is genuinely absent; the line says which part is missing · 🟢 **in `main`** — the change is there and nothing is outstanding, **however it was shaped**: a port merged in a corrected or different form is 🟢, not 🟡, because the icon reports the outcome, not the resemblance. **No icon means not in `main`** — that is the default and is never marked; where absence has actually been verified, say so in words. Beware the near-homonym: "only part of it is **wanted**" is a verdict reason (Decision), not a presence claim — 🟡 is only for what is **already there**. It states a fact about the code, never about PRs or intentions: "in a PR" is `[x]` plus the link, and "diff before porting" is the verdict `take, not as written`. 🟢 is **deliberately redundant**: it can always be derived — from a merged carrier, or from the `main`-side commit *Evidence* already requires. It is kept because this list is read by people, and "already landed" must be visible at a glance rather than resolved from PR states one line at a time. So 🟢 is a **reading promoted to a mark**, and the only one. **⏳ is retired** — it meant "in a PR and not merged", which is exactly `[x]` with no icon, so it carried no fact of its own (`[x]` + 🟢 means merged). A **delegated** line carries no icon: its state is tracked on [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29).

**Evidence for "already in `main`".** Any claim that a change is already in `main` — the 🟢 icon, or a `drop — already in `main`` verdict, on **either** tracker — must name the evidence: the `main`-side commit, the merged PR that brought it, or the file and symbol where it lives — cited as *Citations* requires. An unreferenced claim is a guess, not a verdict. *Not yet merged is not `main`.* A change waiting in an open or draft PR has no evidence to name here: its verdict is `drop — superseded`, pointing at that PR, not `drop — already in `main``.

**Marks must not contradict.** Every mark asserts something: the **box** says what is *owed* (`[ ]` a PR, `[x]` a merge, `-` nothing), the **icon** says where the code *is*, the **verdict** says what we decided, **`needs`** says what must come first. Read a line's marks together as one sentence before writing it; if that sentence contradicts itself, one half is wrong — and which half is a question about the code, not about the wording. Keeping the dimensions separate is what makes them cross-checkable. *Two that bite:* 🟢 says nothing is owed, so it takes `[x]` or a plain point and **never `[ ]`** (🟡 leaves the rest owed, so it takes any box); and carriage is asserted by the box alone, so prose naming a carrier beside a `[ ]` means the box or the prose is wrong.

**Claims about other lines must hold there too.** A pointer, a carrier, a twin or a delegation asserts something about a **second** line; the assertion is a defect unless that line bears it out. Four readings follow, and each is checkable:
1. **Ownership terminates.** Exactly one line holds the substantive verdict for a change. Following the pointers must reach it — and the chain must never return to where it started. A line that hands its verdict to another must not be handed it back.
2. **A pointer is total.** A delegation marker names **every** item that claims that commit, not the first one found; an item it omits is orphaned from the decision meant to cover it.
3. **A carrier carries.** A PR named by `[x]` must actually settle the line — contain the change, or, on a `drop — superseded`, remove the need for it. A PR that never had it, or has since dropped it, is not a carrier — see *Checkbox*.
4. **A counterpart names you back.** Where one tracker names its twin, the other names it too; a mapping asserted on one side only is unverified.

**⚠ is emphasis, never status.** It highlights a caveat in prose — a conflict, a risk, a stale measurement. It is not a verdict, not an icon, and never the only place a fact lives: whatever it emphasises must also sit in its proper field.

**Sequencing.** A dependency is written **`needs <id>`** — the id of the item that must land first — **once per line, with every prerequisite named inside that one mark** (`needs `141` `103` `508``), in that exact form; prose such as "rides", "requires" or "prerequisite" is not a substitute, though it may stay as explanation. Nothing marks *blocked*: that is **read** from `needs <id>` plus the state of the item it names, so it cannot go stale. "What can start now" is every `[ ]` line with no `needs`, or whose `needs` target is already merged. An item **never `needs` the PR that carries it** — that PR is its vehicle (the checkbox link), not a prerequisite; it ships *with* it, not *after* it.

**Record findings, not the record's history.** A *finding* about the artifact stays — "mapping to `X` disproven (0%)", "already on `main` as `Y`" — because it stops someone re-deriving it. How the **entry** got here does not: "moved from Bucket 5", "re-triaged from Bucket 1", "corrects an earlier note". The issue's own edit history holds that, and the line's current position already says where it sits.

**Derived state is read, not recorded.** If a fact can be read from something else — a PR's state, another item's verdict, whether a `needs` target has landed — do **not** write it down. A recorded state is stale the moment the thing it describes moves; a reading is always current. This is *Say it once* extended over time, and it is why ⏳, 🔴 and a *blocked* mark were all rejected. The one deliberate exception is 🟢 — a derived fact kept as a mark for scanning; see *Status icon*. Nothing else earns that.

**Date what you establish.** A measurement or a manual verification records **when** it was made and **against what** — the `main` or `devel` commit it was checked against. An undated claim cannot be refreshed or trusted, only re-done. Say "date unknown" rather than leave it implied.

**Citations resolve, or they are defects.** Every commit hash, PR number and item id cited anywhere must exist and must name what it claims to name. A hash that resolves to no commit, a `needs` pointing at no line, or two abbreviations of one commit on two lines is a defect to fix — not a note to keep. Compare hashes by their full object id, not by the text. *Cite an artifact by its permanent id; cite a place in code by file and symbol.* An **artifact** — a commit, a patch, a PR — is named by its id, never by where it sits. A **place in code** is named by file and symbol (`lib/loadhosts.c`, `xmh_item()`); a line number may accompany that but never replace it, and it is a measurement like any other, so it carries the date and the commit it was read against. A bare `file:line` is a defect — line numbers shift on every edit, and a reader cannot tell a stale one from a live one. *Cite the permanent id.* A commit is citable once it is reachable from `main` or `devel`. A sha on a PR branch is not: the branch rebases and the id dies — silently, and only for other readers, since it still resolves in the clone that wrote it. Cite the PR for work in flight, and the permanent commit once one exists. *Citing a tracked artifact outside the tracker.* A pull request porting one of these names it in its title, in the single trailing parenthesis: the Terabithia patch first, then the `devel` commit, separated by a comma — `(TBT 214, devel f106d8840)`. Several ids on one side are joined by `/` and listed, not summarised — `(TBT 138/222/226/227, devel 01f79009/a1718cd7/0e7d1962)`. Where only one side exists, only that side is named. The abbreviation is the one this tracker uses, so a commit reads the same in both places.

**Define a slot by what may occupy it, never by what may not.** A closed list of allowed values excludes everything else by construction; a second list of forbidden values can only restate it or contradict it. (This retired the old *Not verdicts* blacklist, which had come to forbid a reason the Verdict clause allows.)

**Grouping — by subject, for reading and for delivery.** Lines that will produce work are grouped by **subject** — the part of Xymon the change touches (xymonlaunch, proxy, RRD, channel/IPC, history, client, web, lib, build …) — not by where the patch came from or how it was measured. Subject grouping is what makes the list readable and lets PRs and commits be organised by subject. It does **not** imply one PR per group: a subject may take several PRs (xymonlaunch took [#219](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/219) → [#172](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/172) → [#173](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/173)), and one PR may span several subjects — but only as far as one reviewer can still read it in a sitting. A bundle of 25 commits across a dozen areas drew no review in seven months and was closed unmerged; the narrowest coherent PR is the one that lands. Dropped lines need no subject grouping and stay together. **Exactly two levels of grouping: the bucket, and the phase under it.** A bucket (`#` heading) is a **disposition** — what we have decided to do with everything inside. A **phase** is the only thing that may sit under it: one heading, its members below it, closed by the first blank line after them. **Nothing nests under a phase** — no family, no sub-group, no second roster. A subject, a commit family and a delivery step are all phases; what tells them apart is their name, not their depth. When a phase grows a would-be sub-group, **promote it to its own phase, never indent it** — a third level is where joint claims hide: it reads as belonging to the parent's members too, and it gives a member somewhere to keep its verdict other than its own line.

**Group headings.** A bucket or family heading may carry any statement true of **every** line under it — a delegation, the family PR, a shared blocker or risk — and those lines need not repeat it. The **verdict always stays on the line**, so a line reads on its own. A line that contradicts its heading is in the wrong group: **move it.** **A heading names no PR — none, ever.** Not a member's carrier, not the family's own PR, not a shared risk, not the delivery chain. A heading carries the **subject** and nothing that can go stale: PR numbers move, merge, close and get superseded, and a heading is the one place nobody re-reads when they do. Whatever a PR reference would say belongs on the **lines** it is true of — the line is the unit of work, and whoever ports it reads it alone — or, where it is genuinely about the whole section, in that section's **prose**, which is read as prose and revised as prose. A group-level PR claim is also usually a **joint** one, true of the group but of no member exactly, which hides that members differ. A heading over delegated lines may carry the family's shared facts (its PR, blocker or risk); **per-change** analysis still lives on the [#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29) line. **The first blank line after the members closes the group**; one directly under the heading, where Markdown convention puts it, does not. Everything between the heading and the closing blank line is a member, and the count in the heading must match. Items that follow are their own artifacts, not members.

**[#29](https://github.com/xymon-monitoring/xymon/issues/29)'s Audit checklist** at the end is a plain progress list, where `[x]` just means done.
